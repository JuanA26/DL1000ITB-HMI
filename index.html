RPM Sweep — Bode Plot

Closed-loop RPM sweep -- logs accel amplitude and phase at each RPM setpoint across the grid. Pick the Undamped case (PCB1 mode r, reverse-cycle sweep that dodges the sharp resonance) or the Damped case (mode r2, a straight low-to-high pass for the broadened resonance -- requires the damper fitted). A full sweep takes several minutes; Stop aborts early and keeps whatever points were already collected. Save the finished run to a file, then load previous runs back in to overlay them here for comparison (e.g. undamped vs damped).

Damping case

Hover a point on either plot for its exact RPM/amplitude or RPM/phase reading (MATLAB-style data cursor). The dashed vertical line marks the resonance frequency above, converted to RPM (Hz x 60). Scroll to zoom in/out around the cursor, drag with the left mouse button to pan around, double-click to reset the view.

Phase of the 1x response relative to the encoder pulse: how far after the trigger the accel peak lands, as a fraction of one revolution. It runs 0° well below resonance, crosses 90° (dashed line) at the natural frequency, and approaches 180° above it -- the 90° crossing locates resonance more sharply than the amplitude peak. Points are auto-zeroed against the sub-1000 RPM average to remove the fixed mounting offset (raw values are kept in Save Run). Faded points had too weak a 1x tone to trust the phase.

Data Log Summary — Reading Phase by Hand

PCB1 recorded 5 s at the full ~2330 SPS while holding the RPM; the statistics below use all of it, and the plot shows the middle 1 s. The gold vertical lines are encoder pulses — one per revolution, fired as the unbalance mass passes the inductive sensor.

Measure to the trough, not the peak. The encoder fires when the beam is at maximum displacement — but this sensor measures acceleration, and a = −ω²x, so the accel trace is upside-down relative to displacement. Max displacement therefore shows up as the accel trace's lowest point. The trough is where the beam actually is.

To find the phase angle by hand: pick a gold pulse line, find the next trough of the accel trace after it, and read off the time gap Δt (hover the plot for exact values). One revolution takes T = 60/RPM seconds. The phase angle is then 360° × Δt / T. Well below resonance the trough sits almost on the pulse (≈0°); at resonance it lags a quarter turn (90°); above resonance, half a turn (180°). Scroll to zoom in on a couple of cycles, drag to pan, double-click to reset.

The big number is what PCB1's software lock-in measured from the same capture — the same quantity you just read (pulse → trough, as a fraction of a revolution), but computed from all ~11650 samples and every pulse at once instead of one trough and one line. A few degrees of disagreement is normal and expected: eyeballing a trough is imprecise precisely because the waveform is flattest there.

Where the raw number comes from: the lock-in measures pulse → accel peak, so it reads ≈180° more than your displacement reading — that's the a = −ω²x inversion, subtracted out above. Any further constant offset is sensor mounting angle, which can't be known from a single capture. That's exactly why the Bode sweep's phase plot normalises: it takes several far-below-resonance points, calls their average 0°, and subtracts — giving the clean 0 → 90 → 180 curve across the whole RPM range without ever needing to know the mounting angle.

Bump Test

Triggered acquisition (PCB1 mode b) -- arms, waits for a physical tap/bump exceeding the threshold, captures the decay at full ~2330 SPS, then plots the time response and its FFT here. Capture can't be aborted once triggered (same as a full vibration capture) -- only the armed/waiting phase can. Hover either plot for an exact reading (MATLAB-style data cursor); the FFT plot also auto-marks its top peaks with frequency labels. Scroll to zoom in/out around the cursor, drag with the left mouse button to pan around, double-click to reset the view.

Free Vibration — Natural Frequency & Damping

Untriggered raw-accelerometer capture (PCB1 freevib) with the motor off. Deflect the beam and let go right as you press Start Record — the trace streams in live as the beam rings down (~2.2–2.5 Hz). Measure the natural frequency (peak spacing → period) and logarithmic-decrement damping (how fast successive peaks shrink) with the data cursor: hover the time plot for exact (t, accel) readings, scroll to zoom, drag to pan, double-click to reset. The FFT below marks the dominant frequency. The plotted trace is low-pass filtered (zero-phase, so peaks keep their exact time and height) to strip sensor noise for clean hand measurement — adjust the cutoff or tick Show raw to compare; the untouched raw data is what gets saved.

Automated estimate: peaks are detected on the ring-down, the damped period gives the frequency, and a straight-line fit of ln(peak amplitude) vs. cycle number gives the logarithmic decrement δ, from which ζ = δ / √(4π² + δ²) and the undamped fₙ = f_d / √(1 − ζ²). Treat it as a cross-check against your own cursor reading, not ground truth — a short or noisy ring-down weakens the fit.
